The foil

A wing under the water, wings in your head.

Flight, in silence

Like an airplane, beneath the surface

The foil is made up of a front wing and a stabiliser connected to the board by a mast. At just a few knots of speed, the lift raises you up: you ride one metre above the water — no noise, no effort, even in very light wind.

Frequently asked questions

Flight, in questions.

Do I need to know how to sail before starting foiling?

No. That's exactly what the towed simulator is for: no wing to handle, the boat provides the traction, and you focus on balance and flight. Many people fly on their very first session.

Is foiling dangerous?

It's a smooth ride that's easy on the body when properly supervised: helmet, impact vest, a suitable stretch of water and gradual progression are all part of the Coriolis method.

Wing foil or kite foil — which should I choose?

The wing is simpler to get going and perfect on the lagoons; kite foil offers more speed and finesse, but requires you to already be independent on a twintip kiteboard.

What happens if there's no wind?

That's the beauty of wake foil: the boat replaces the wind. No-wind days become flying days.
